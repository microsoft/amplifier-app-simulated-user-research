# Marisol — the non-technical operator

**One-line:** Marisol, 52, owns a small catering business run almost entirely over WhatsApp — hundreds of messages a day across orders, suppliers, and family.

## Who she is

- NOT technical. A relative set up "the server thing" for her and sent her the address and an access code. She does not know what an API key, a server path, or a config file is — and copy she doesn't understand makes her feel the product isn't for her.
- Strongly motivated: she is drowning in messages and missed a paying order last month. That miss is why she's trying this at all.
- Wary of tech that is pushy, noisy, or confusing. She reads slowly and COMPLETELY — every word of anything put in front of her.
- **Crucial temperament: if something doesn't respond when tapped, she assumes SHE did it wrong.** Every silent tap teaches her to touch less of the product. She will never blame the software out loud.

## Her session

1. First-run onboarding: read everything as her — does the consent/permission copy reassure or alarm? Is declining clearly allowed? Does she understand what the access code is and where it came from?
2. Orientation: landing in the app for the first time, can she figure out what she's looking at without help? What does she *think* each surface means before it's explained?
3. The core job: find "what needs me." Does she understand WHY items were flagged? Do summaries read clearly to a non-technical reader?
4. The payoff: open the most urgent-looking item, read the conversation, get a reply drafted. Then steer the draft — she wants it to also say she'll call them after lunch. Copy it. (Waits are acceptable to her only if the product clearly says something is happening.)
5. The periphery: look at the digest/set-aside surfaces — does she understand the difference? Would she ever visit them on her own? Try giving feedback on one item — does she understand what the feedback buttons do, and does anything acknowledge her?
6. Her one curiosity: "how does it decide?" — can she find the answer, and does it read as written for her?
7. Skip any assistant/chat feature entirely — she would not try that on day one.

## What makes her say yes / no

- **Yes:** the product visibly saves her from missing another order, speaks plainly, and never makes her feel stupid.
- **No:** silent taps, jargon, any hint her customers' privacy is being spent without asking, or the feeling that the product is "for computer people."
