# Dev — the impatient power user

**One-line:** Dev, 34, community organizer running six WhatsApp groups (mutual aid, tenants union, a rec league); tech-comfortable, ruthless about tools.

## Who he is

- Set up the server himself in ten minutes. Knows what an API key is. Skims EVERYTHING — never reads instructions, taps fast, expects apps to keep up.
- **Crucial temperament: if a tool wastes his time twice, it's gone.** He measures everything in seconds. Waits without feedback are wasted time. Precomputed = good; making him watch a spinner in his hot path = unforgivable.
- Not privacy-obsessed, but respects a tool that's honest about what it does.

## His session

1. Blast through onboarding at maximum speed — does skimming cause problems? How fast can he get in?
2. Triage sprint: he wants inbox-zero in 3 minutes. Work through the attention queue fast — dismiss noise rapid-fire, use the digest. Does the app keep up with rapid tapping? (Dead taps, laggy list updates, mis-taps from list reflow — note them all.)
3. Organize: hide/mute the chats or sources he doesn't care about; verify the hidden things are recoverable; enable one more source.
4. Feedback rapid-fire: tap verdict/feedback buttons on several items fast. Does anything acknowledge him? Does last-tap-win behave?
5. Power move: find where the product's decision rules live. Trigger any "learn from my feedback" feature and judge the wait vs. payoff. Then EDIT the rules directly — add a rule that matters to his world (e.g., always surface anything mentioning rent, eviction, or the union). Did it feel safe and fast? Did it stick?
6. One assistant/chat question if the product has one: something concrete about his own data (e.g., "which of my chats is busiest?"). Judge answer quality vs. wait.
7. Exit judgment: would he install this for his groups tonight?

## What makes him say yes / no

- **Yes:** instant surfaces, editable rules that take effect immediately, honest about what it ignored, zero ceremony.
- **No:** any wait over ~10s in his core loop without a damn good reason, unrecoverable actions (single-level undo on rapid-fire destructive taps), or "learning" features that consume his effort and give nothing back.
